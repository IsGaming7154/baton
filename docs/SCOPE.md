# What is built, and what is not

This is a hackathon project. The honest scope:

**Built**

- `:director` — the arbitration state machine. Complete: two priority classes, preemption, the fit-or-drop admission rule, guard interval, seek rebaselining, pause-versus-stall handling, floor timeouts, adaptive duck depth, the gap between voices. 124 tests, including property tests over 1,300 randomised timelines.
- `:player` — ExoPlayer adapter. Plays the clip, reports position and playback state, ducks the content, and meters the content's loudness live in the render chain.
- `:speech` — text-to-speech engine selection, rendering to WAV, the intelligibility chain, loudness normalisation, and `AudioTrack` playback with observed (not timed) completion.
- `:loudness` — ITU-R BS.1770-4 in full: K-weighting, gated integrated LUFS, a sliding short-term meter, a 4× polyphase true-peak meter.
- `:app` — the tracer. One bundled clip, one bundled description track of six description cues, a bundled English subtitle sidecar parsed into spoken-subtitle cues and rendered on-screen through Media3, the whole timeline logged under a single tag. A transport bar — Rewind, Play/Pause, Fast-forward — and, one press below it, an access-services row — Description, Description length, Spoken subtitles, Captions, Suppress description, Screen curtain — are the whole UI: each focus change is spoken as `UI_NARRATION` through the director, ducking the film, and is cut off by the next press. Turning Description or Spoken subtitles off makes the director pass that kind's cues over. Description is stored per title and starts off for a title never switched on; Suppress mutes it for the session without changing what is stored. Description length Short makes the director take each description's shortest variant from the next cue on. The curtain blacks out the picture and leaves the sound alone. The row's phrases are rendered on the preparation thread after the film starts, so a toggle focused in the first ~8 s of playback is silent (logged `not-rendered`). Preparation itself renders every voice to audio before the film may begin, and says so on screen and out loud rather than leaving a black screen. Every rendered file is kept and reused on the next launch, keyed by the text, the engine, the voice and the speaking rate, so only the first start waits. Measured on the Stick from the release build, 2026-09-22: a first launch renders 50 files in 17.6 s and reaches the film at 20.8 s; the next launch reads all 50 back in 183 ms. It does that without waiting for the text-to-speech engine: the last launch wrote down which engine and voice rendered the files, so they are found by that while the engine binds alongside, and only something not yet rendered waits for it. Measured back to back on 2026-09-28, five warm launches each, from the system's `START` to `playback starting`: the film at a median 2.96 s where it had been 3.94 s, with the audio ready at 0.92 s instead of 3.91 s. What is left is the application's own first frame, which the film cannot start before.
- `:pipeline` — the offline track builder, complete and exercised end to end against recorded fixtures shaped like real Amazon Transcribe, Nova and Polly responses, with live adapters for Transcribe, Polly and Gemini that have each run against the real service.

The tracer has been run on a Fire TV Stick, and the device measurements quoted throughout this document come from those runs; they are labelled where they appear. Everything else here was verified from the repository itself.

**Designed, specified, not built**

- The live AWS pipeline. `:pipeline`'s ports are defined against Transcribe, Bedrock Nova and Polly, and its fixtures are stored in those services' own response shapes so the parsers are the real ones. Polly and Transcribe have live adapters so far (`PollySynthesis` and `TranscribeTranscription`, on the AWS SDK for Java v2): they are tested against fake clients. Polly's manual smoke call (`gradlew :pipeline:pollySmoke`) has been run once, billed 56 characters, exactly the estimate; Transcribe's (`gradlew :pipeline:transcribeSmoke`) was first refused on the account's Free plan (friction-log entry 20) and, after the account moved to a paid plan, ran once on the demo clip: 62 German words, every one inside the timing of one of the clip's 12 subtitle lines, parsed by the same parser the recorded fixtures use. Nova has a live adapter too (`NovaDrafting`, Bedrock `Converse` with the clip sent inline and the model id a required parameter, e.g. the `us.amazon.nova-2-lite-v1:0` inference profile), tested against a fake client with its billed tokens settling the spend guard's estimate; its manual smoke run (`gradlew :pipeline:novaSmoke`) has never succeeded: AWS declined Bedrock access for this account until it has more usage history (friction-log entry 21). `GeminiDrafting` drafts in its place, on the Gemini API's free tier, and its smoke run (`gradlew :pipeline:geminiSmoke`) has run on the demo clip, reading the key from `GEMINI_API_KEY` or `~/.baton/.env`. What has not run is all three chained in one live pass. Nothing in the test suite makes a network call. This is deliberate: the loop that has to be right is the one that runs for free.
- Reactive speech beyond menu narration. The director arbitrates `VQA_ANSWER` today, and its preemption behaviour is tested, but nothing in the app raises one yet. There is no question ring and no visual question answering.
- Spoken subtitles from a *live* subtitle track. The kind exists and outranks description in the policy, and the app now parses and speaks a bundled subtitle sidecar (`SubtitleFile.kt`, `TrackPreparation.kt`) the same way it speaks the bundled description track; what remains unbuilt is deriving that subtitle track from the live pipeline, rather than authoring it in advance.
- Description frequency and emphasis settings: cut. The description speaking rate independent of VoiceView's is built (Slower, Normal, Faster on the access-services row, stored across restarts), and applies from the next start because speech is rendered and measured at preparation. Specified in [`PRD.md`](../PRD.md). The screen curtain and the access-services row are built and were checked on the Stick from its log and screenshots; neither has yet been run with VoiceView on.

**Not possible, and why it is worth saying**

Baton arbitrates the voices it owns. VoiceView runs outside the process and exposes no signal that it is speaking, so Baton cannot schedule around it; it can only get out of its way. VoiceView asks for transient audio focus before it speaks, and Baton holds focus in a way that makes the framework tell it (friction-log entry 19). While VoiceView has the floor, Baton cuts what it is saying, starts nothing new, drops descriptions whose moment passes and refuses button narration; on the Stick its voice was released 24 ms after VoiceView took focus. That yields; it does not coordinate. A documented "VoiceView is speaking" signal is still the gap the feature requests in friction-log entries 1 and 19 ask Fire OS to close.

## Known gaps

Open, and not hidden:

- **An overrun is bounded, not eliminated.** The director withdraws the floor from an utterance that has run past the silence window it was granted, or past its speaker deadline -- modelled as `Speaking.Withdrawn` carrying a `Withdrawal` whose `WithdrawalReason` is `OutOfSilence`, `SpeakerOverdue`, or `NeverMeasured` (`Directive.kt`). `Tracer` acts on it by calling `speaker.stop(id)` and logs an `ANOMALY floor withdrawn` line carrying both `measuredMs` and `heldForMs`, so the price of the policy is legible afterwards. What is not bounded is the withdrawal's own latency: the overrun is the 50 ms director tick plus however long the platform's `AudioTrack` takes to actually go quiet after `stop()` -- and that second term has never been measured on the device. Baton does not claim a zero-overlap guarantee. It claims a bounded and reported one.
- **VoiceView inside Baton, device-checked 2026-09-16 and 2026-09-18.** With the transport buttons `invisibleToUser`, VoiceView stayed silent on focus moves in the first run, but spoke on its own at playback start and at each screen-curtain toggle, and in the second run for about 10 s after a Down press into the access-services row. What it says is not recorded anywhere a developer can reach. Baton now yields to it (above); whether the first few milliseconds of VoiceView still overlap Baton's cut needs an audio capture of the Stick's output, which this project does not yet have (friction-log entry 15).
- The live AWS pipeline, visual question answering, the question ring, spoken subtitles from a live track, and the accessible UI surfaces beyond the transport bar are all specified and unbuilt. See above.
